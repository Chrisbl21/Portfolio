Project Proposal: Graphic Design Portfolio

Prepared by Christopher Blank

Purpose of the Website

The purpose of this website is to present my work as a graphic designer and help me secure an entry-level graphic design position or internship. The site will act as my professional home online: it will show my best projects, explain my design process, and make it easy for employers to contact me.

Target Audience

The site is written for the people who decide who gets interviewed:

  • Hiring managers and creative directors at design studios and agencies
  • In-house marketing and brand teams looking for designers
  • Recruiters who screen candidates before a design review
  • Freelance clients who need branding, print, or digital design

These visitors are busy and often review many portfolios in a row, so the site needs to be fast to scan, visually polished, and easy to navigate.

Research: What My Portfolio Needs

I researched what design professionals say employers look for. The findings below shaped the components I plan to include.

Findings

  • Show the work, not just a description of it. Design hiring managers at Adobe say they look for portfolios that show rather than tell, and they point out that template-driven portfolios can all look alike even when the work is very different (Source 1).
  • Explain the process and thinking. A graphic design tutor at University of the Arts London advises that a portfolio must communicate creative process and design thinking even when the designer is not in the room to explain it (Source 2).
  • Treat the portfolio like a marketing site and keep it focused. A design hiring manager writing for ADPList recommends treating the portfolio as a marketing site, tailoring it to the job, and avoiding portfolios that are too long or all look the same (Source 3).

Components My Portfolio Needs

Portfolio components and why each one matters
Component Purpose
Home page with short introduction Quickly states who I am, what I design, and shows a featured project.
Projects gallery Large, high-quality images of my best 4 to 6 pieces (branding, print, digital).
Case study write-ups Explains the problem, my process, and the result for key projects.
Skills and tools page Lists software (such as Adobe Illustrator, Photoshop, InDesign, Figma) and design skills.
Resume page Gives education and experience in a scannable format with a downloadable copy.
About page Adds personality and shows my story and design approach.
Contact page Provides email, LinkedIn, and a contact form so employers can reach me.
Responsive, accessible design Works on phones and desktops, since reviewers may check it on any device.

Planned Site Structure

The site will have at least five pages: Home, Projects, Skills, Resume, and Contact. The site map is included in my project submission. Files will be organized in a root folder with separate css, js, and assets folders.
